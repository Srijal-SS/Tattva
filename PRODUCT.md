# Product

Tattva: a learning layer beyond academics for Indian children. Written from the team's problem statement, business notes, Q&A prep and poster (2026-11-25).

## Platform
web (responsive; phones and laptops at home)

## Stack
Next.js 16 App Router, React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind v4, framer-motion. Demo state lives in localStorage; Supabase comes later.

## Users
- **Primary user:** children aged 10–14 in urban India, digitally comfortable, using it alone or beside a parent.
- **Customer (hypothesis):** their parents, who already pay for supplementary learning and care about development beyond marks.
- **Secondary:** teachers and schools (later, B2B2C); content reviewers via admin.

## Product Purpose
Schools systematically teach *what to know*; far less systematically *what to do, why, how my behaviour affects others, and where I come from*. Tattva makes ethical reasoning, civic sense, cultural understanding and life skills an intentional, continuous practice.

## Positioning
"Learning how to think, decide and participate, not just what to remember." Not a digital textbook, not a video library: a sequence of real situations where the child chooses, sees consequences and compares perspectives. Curated video supports a lesson; it never is the lesson.

## Operating Context
Short sessions (5–10 min) after school or on weekends. Often on a parent's phone. The child arrives curious, not assigned; if it feels like homework, they leave.

## Capabilities and Constraints
- Four themes: Values, Culture, Civic Sense, Life Skills.
- Each lesson: situation → choose → consequence → perspectives → principle → optional video → reflect → real-world challenge.
- No social feed, chat, AI tutor, leaderboards against other children or ads in the MVP.
- Reflections stay private to the child (parents see skills practised, not the text).

## Brand Commitments
- Teach how to reason, never what to believe. Where reasonable people disagree, show the disagreement.
- Culture content labels every claim as fact, interpretation, tradition or belief, notes the region, and cites its source.
- India is diverse: one region's tradition is never presented as "Indian culture".
- No moralising tone (the reason School Cinema is listed as a poor source).

## Evidence on Hand
No user research yet. Willingness to pay, engagement and retention are untested hypotheses. Good source: Kids Pharmer. Competitors: School Cinema, Life Skills 360 (direct); Sensei, EVERFI, Khan Academy Kids, YouTube, books and family (indirect).

## Product Principles
1. Agency first: the child decides before being told anything.
2. Consequences, not verdicts: show what happens and to whom.
3. Meaningful sessions over screen time.
4. Trust is a feature: sources and labels are visible, not buried.
5. Respect the child's intelligence: no baby UI, no lecturing.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.2 AA. 18px minimum for story text, plain-language copy at a Class 5–8 reading level, keyboard and screen-reader support, reduced-motion support, read-aloud for lesson text. Names, places and situations drawn from across India.

## Measurement
We record what children do (opened, chose, finished, wrote a reflection, tried the challenge), never what they write. Choice splits are for conversation, not grading: no choice is the right one.
